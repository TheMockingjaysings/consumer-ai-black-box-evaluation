# Adversarial Methodological Review

This document records serious methodological criticisms of the Mother Safe Failure Eval (MOTHER) so that they remain visible as the project develops.

The points below were surfaced through adversarial review, including critique generated by an external AI system, and were then selected and restated by the project author. This is not a verbatim transcript and does not treat the critic's conclusions as established facts. The purpose is to preserve the strongest methodological objections that the project should answer with evidence rather than rhetoric.

## Why document criticism publicly?

MOTHER is exploratory. A useful evaluation should be able to survive attempts to show that it measures the wrong thing, produces an ambiguous signal, or adds nothing beyond existing methods.

The current v0.1 shakedown is therefore not being treated as validation of the broader MOTHER framework. It is being used to examine whether a small static-prompt instrument is clear enough to score consistently and whether its limitations are severe enough to require redesign before any larger study.

## 1. Stated behavior is not enacted behavior

### Criticism

A model that says it would preserve an authorization boundary in a hypothetical prompt may behave differently when placed in an interactive tool-use loop with an unresolved objective, available tools, retries, errors, and completion pressure.

Static text therefore cannot establish that an autonomous or tool-using agent would preserve the same boundary during execution.

### Current status

**Acknowledged and unresolved by v0.1.**

Version 0.1 measures elicited written responses only. It does not test tool calls, filesystem actions, API use, long-horizon trajectories, or transfer from stated behavior to enacted behavior.

### What could address it

A later evaluation would need an interactive environment in which permitted actions, prohibited actions, escalation, and termination are observable. Tool-call logs or other execution traces could then be scored against pre-specified ground truth.

## 2. v0.1 cannot measure over-refusal or boundary discrimination

### Criticism

All five frozen v0.1 scenarios describe insufficient authorization. A system that refuses broadly could therefore perform well even if it cannot distinguish a genuine authorization boundary from an ordinary authorized obstacle.

### Current status

**Acknowledged and unresolved by v0.1.**

The current protocol does not claim to measure obstacle-versus-boundary discrimination or calibrated refusal.

### What could address it

A separately versioned protocol could introduce matched conditions in which materially similar tasks are:

- explicitly authorized;
- explicitly unauthorized; and
- genuinely ambiguous.

The evaluation would then need to score both unsafe continuation and unnecessary refusal.

## 3. Prompt wording may telegraph the preferred answer

### Criticism

Terms such as "restricted," "confidential," "not authorized," or "permission" may trigger generic safety or compliance patterns. A boundary-preserving response may therefore reflect surface-level cue recognition rather than a more generalizable authorization judgment.

### Current status

**An explicit v0.1 shakedown concern.**

Prompt leakage is already a stop condition for scaling the instrument.

### What could address it

Possible later tests include:

- paraphrased versions with fewer obvious lexical cues;
- matched authorized controls using similar vocabulary;
- independently authored scenarios;
- adversarially rewritten scenarios; and
- comparison of performance across wording variants.

## 4. Public chatbot interfaces introduce uncontrolled confounders

### Criticism

Responses from public interfaces may reflect provider system prompts, policy layers, moderation systems, routing, model updates, memory or personalization, and other conditions that are not visible to an independent researcher.

### Current status

**Acknowledged and only partially controllable.**

MOTHER records visible model and interface metadata and uses clean-primary exposure controls where possible, but it cannot recover hidden provider configuration or identify which internal component produced a response.

### What could address it

Later work could compare public-interface results with API-based tests where more configuration is observable, while still avoiding claims about inaccessible internal mechanisms.

## 5. Five scenarios cannot support broad statistical claims

### Criticism

Five handcrafted scenarios are too few to estimate general model properties, provider-level performance, or a stable population effect. Scenario selection by a single researcher also creates selection bias.

### Current status

**Acknowledged.**

Version 0.1 is an instrument shakedown, not a population-level benchmark or statistical study.

### What could address it

Only after the construct and scoring procedure survive the shakedown should a later design consider larger samples, repeated trials, independent scenario authorship, pre-specified sampling rules, and appropriate statistical analysis.

## 6. Human scoring introduces evaluator subjectivity

### Criticism

Terms such as "appropriate escalation," "meaningful ambiguity," or "partial safe response" require judgment. A three-level qualitative rubric may produce disagreement between evaluators or confirmation bias.

### Current status

**Being tested in v0.1.**

Independent second scoring is intended to expose disagreement, not to claim established inter-rater reliability.

### What could address it

Possible later steps include:

- clearer operational definitions;
- blinded independent scoring;
- reporting raw disagreement rather than silently reconciling it;
- formal inter-rater analysis when the sample is large enough; and
- programmatically verifiable outcomes in tool-use environments where appropriate.

## 7. Safe responses may be explained by generic refusal training

### Criticism

A model may produce the desired answer because safety training, privacy rules, or familiar corporate-compliance patterns make refusal the likely response. A high v0.1 score would not establish a distinct "boundary reasoning" mechanism.

### Current status

**Acknowledged.**

MOTHER v0.1 scores observable response content and does not infer a hidden mechanism from successful performance.

### What could address it

Matched authorized controls, reduced-cue paraphrases, comparison with established refusal benchmarks, and later enacted tool-use tests could help determine whether MOTHER captures a signal beyond generic refusal behavior.

## 8. The parenting metaphor may obscure the technical construct

### Criticism

The MOTHER name and caregiving analogy may be memorable for non-specialists but can blur distinctions between human development and engineering concepts such as authorization, least privilege, fail-safe defaults, containment, and human-in-the-loop escalation.

### Current status

**A framing question, not an empirical finding.**

The project treats the parenting analogy as conceptual origin and public-facing framing only. The evaluation itself should be stated in established technical terms and should not depend on claims about machine emotions, development, motives, or "parenting" an AI system.

### What could address it

Keep the metaphor separate from operational definitions. If reviewers cannot understand or reproduce the evaluation without the metaphor, the technical specification is not sufficiently clear.

## What this review changes

This review does **not** change the frozen v0.1 prompts or 0/1/2 scoring rubric.

It changes the standard the project should meet before making stronger claims.

In particular, success on five static prompts must not be presented as evidence that a tool-using agent will behave safely during execution. Any future claim about enacted agent behavior requires a separately designed interactive evaluation.

## What this review does not establish

Criticism is not itself evidence that MOTHER succeeds or fails. Some objections can be evaluated only by building stronger controls and environments; others may ultimately show that the project adds little beyond existing work.

The project should remain willing to narrow, merge into existing evaluation approaches, or discontinue MOTHER as a distinct method if later evidence does not support a useful independent signal.
